# CHAPTER FOUR
## Data presentation analysis and discussion
### 4.0 Introduction
This chapter evaluates the rebuilt Meter Tracking System and automated Head-End System (HES) integration model. The main finding is that the prototype correctly handled all 80 commissioning scenarios and passed all 54 core functional checks. These results exceeded the 90% target for correct functional behaviour in the selected development suite. They do not establish 90% predictive accuracy or a 90% connection rate on the ZETDC network.
The model's task is to receive a scanned meter identifier, validate installation details, request connection to the appropriate HES and record its response. A failed or uncertain operation must leave the meter unconnected. The HES team then completes manual commissioning and supplies a confirmation that can be verified. No logistic regression, ridge regression or delay-prediction training is used in this rebuilt process.
The chapter follows the five objectives in Chapter One. The supplied consumer workbook provides context for Objectives 1 and 2. Application evidence addresses Objectives 3 and 4. Objective 5 is considered through the proposed future state and the expert and operational evidence still required. The new local software evaluation took place on 11 October 2026.
The evaluated implementation uses a three-day department limit, named worker passwords, three notification channels and manual handling of failed automatic connections. Earlier references to seven days, role PINs, automatic commissioning retries and prediction training do not describe this rebuild. The related Chapter Three methods must be aligned before final submission.
### 4.1 Records and evaluation approach
The supplied workbook contains 600 development records. Its inclusion flag selects 572 records for descriptive analysis and excludes 28. A separate software register contains 100 development meters: 80 contractor-stage cases for commissioning and 20 earlier-stage cases for custody and release tests. These populations serve different purposes and must not be combined into one performance denominator.
Both sources are synthetic development material. They support calculations and repeatable tests, but do not establish the actual distribution of waiting times or faults across ZETDC. The workbook was not relabelled, selectively filtered or altered to manufacture a higher score. The new fixtures provide explicit operating conditions for testing the required commissioning process.
The 80 commissioning scenarios include 60 valid simulator acknowledgements and 20 negative responses, split equally among rejection, timeout, pending acknowledgement and a receipt for the wrong meter. Expected and actual outcomes are retained in the evidence package. These are designed tests, not a random field sample. The same development work produced the implementation and tests, so independent acceptance testing remains necessary.
Functional and integration testing assess behaviour and interactions between components. They answer a different question from predictive accuracy (International Software Testing Qualifications Board [ISTQB], 2024). The evidence includes individual checks, scenario outcomes, manual confirmations, audit events, notification states, browser tests and runtime versions.

### 4.2 Objective 1 Mapping the current meter journey
The mapped journey covers procurement, central stores, Meter Test, transit to depot, depot, installation crew and HES integration. Each stage needs dated events and an identified holder. The rebuilt software authenticates four transfers: Procurement to Stores, Stores to Meter Test, Meter Test to Depot and Depot to Contractor. Transport is not a separate authenticated department in this implementation; this would need an additional custodian and event design.
The recalculated workbook statistics show substantial waiting in the development records. Procurement has the largest median, at 64 days. Central stores, Meter Test, transit, depot and installation crew have medians of 17, 24, 7, 19 and 17 days respectively. The integration-window median is 28 days. These describe the supplied development cases, not newly observed ZETDC performance.

The integration mean of 105.94 days is much higher than its median. There are 106 open integration windows among the included records. Their elapsed ages contribute to the long upper tail. An open age is not a completed duration and may increase further. The figures therefore should not be treated as a forecast for each newly installed meter.
Stage medians must not be added and presented as the median end-to-end journey. A valid end-to-end measure requires paired events for the same complete journeys. This chapter does not carry forward an unsupported end-to-end mean simply because it appeared in the earlier draft.
The design response is a register that retains true entry times and current holders. Reducing the escalation threshold from seven days to three changes the policy, not the recorded dates. The actual time held remains visible even when it greatly exceeds the limit.
### 4.3 Objective 2 The unbilled integration window
The included workbook records total 315,908,190 kWh of estimated unbilled consumption, valued at USD 28,005,440.44 and ZiG 726,034,260.69 under their recorded assumptions. These are gross exposure estimates over different record-specific windows. They are not audited revenue losses or money recovered by the rebuilt application.
The Chapter One illustration of 58.4 GWh over a common 30-day delay is a different calculation. At USD 0.13 per kWh it gives USD 7.592 million. Reducing its assumed window would reduce the illustration proportionately, but would not demonstrate an achieved operational benefit. This chapter does not claim a measured 98% reduction in revenue at risk.
HES connection also does not prove billing or cash collection. The rebuilt model records commissioning confirmation only. It does not claim first-reading acceptance, billing activation, retrospective recovery or realised savings. Those require separate authorised interfaces and reconciliation with actual billing records.
A field pilot should compare installation, confirmed HES connection, first accepted billing data and any interim or retrospective charges for each meter. It should distinguish exposure during the gap from irrecoverable loss. The current software tests cannot provide that financial evidence.
### 4.4 Objective 3 Results for the Meter Tracking System
#### 4.4.1 Custody and password authentication
Users, meters, sessions, notifications and audit events are stored on the server. Local evaluation used SQLite; the deployment package also supports PostgreSQL shared between applications. The Vercel configuration requires PostgreSQL and does not silently treat temporary serverless storage as a permanent register.
Each worker has a named account, department and role. Passwords are stored as salted hashes. Sign-in creates a revocable one-hour session. The releasing worker must re-enter their password, while a distinct receiving worker supplies theirs. The receiving worker must belong to the next department in the chain.
Both signatures are checked before custody changes. Tests rejected incorrect passwords, skipped stages and one worker signing both sides. Four successful handovers moved a new meter from Procurement to Contractor and recorded both worker names. An outdated request could not overwrite a later custody state.
A conditional revision check also protects competing requests. In the concurrent duplicate test, two requests produced exactly one successful transfer. These checks demonstrate selected authentication and custody controls. They do not constitute a complete penetration or production-security assessment.

#### 4.4.2 Three-day escalation and notification channels
Three days is implemented as 72 elapsed hours from departmental entry. At exactly 72 hours a meter is not yet beyond the limit. At 72 hours plus one second it is overdue. Both boundary cases passed. Display rounding does not change the underlying threshold.
The scheduler creates application, email and WhatsApp records for an overdue event. Application items are available in the shared notification view. External messages wait for the dispatch worker. Repeating a sweep does not create duplicate messages for the same departmental entry.
External channels were simulated during this evaluation. Their status is therefore simulated, not delivered. Deliberate sender timeouts left messages failed; later dispatch could retry notification delivery independently of commissioning. Provider acceptance is recorded as accepted and is not represented as recipient delivery.
The package contains SMTP and Twilio WhatsApp adapters, a separate worker and a protected scheduler endpoint. Actual sending requires authorised credentials, recipients and an approved WhatsApp template. A scheduler must run independently of an open browser. No live manager email or WhatsApp delivery was verified.

#### 4.4.3 Tracking acceptance
All 28 tracking checks passed. They covered registration, duplicate rejection, credentials, custody order, two-party approval, time boundaries, channel creation, duplicate suppression, persistence after service restart, concurrent transfers and logout. The selected suite achieved 100% and exceeded its 90% functional target.
The denominator remains important: 28 named checks passed locally. This does not mean that every operational meter is tracked correctly, that all attacks are blocked or that real notifications reached staff. Cloud database behaviour, backup recovery and external delivery remain deployment acceptance activities.

### 4.5 Objective 4 Results for automated HES commissioning
#### 4.5.1 The process model
The model is a rule-based commissioning workflow. It does not learn parameters from data. Scanning retrieves a meter record. The contractor confirms physical installation and enters the installed account and CT ratio. The server verifies contractor permission, custody and the two entered details before requesting connection.
The registered HES routes the request to the Iskra or Secure gateway. A unique request identifier is recorded, and the meter enters processing state. Connected status requires a response naming the same meter and HES, a receipt identifier and a valid confirmation timestamp.
An accepted request without confirmation is insufficient. A timeout is uncertain because the remote system might have acted before its response was lost. Rejected, uncertain and mismatched acknowledgements therefore enter the manual HES queue. Three HES-team notification records are created. No automatic commissioning retry follows.
An interrupted request still processing after 120 seconds is sent for manual reconciliation by the scheduler. This is a prototype recovery threshold, not a utility-approved standard. The HES team must establish the remote status before further action.

#### 4.5.2 Automatic connection and correct handling
All 80 scenarios reached their expected initial state. Sixty positive cases received matching simulator receipts and became connected. Twenty negative cases remained unconnected in the manual queue. The correct-outcome rate was therefore 80/80, or 100%.
Across all attempted cases, the automatic-connection rate was 60/80, or 75%. Among the sixty cases configured to return valid acknowledgements, all sixty connected. These measures answer different questions. Correct handling exceeded the 90% target, but the run does not establish a 90% automatic-connection rate across all cases.
Marking the twenty fault cases connected would increase the displayed connection count while making the system wrong. The evaluation therefore rewards safe handling of negative outcomes rather than treating every unconnected meter as a software defect.
All sixteen commissioning functional checks passed. They covered account and CT mismatch, unissued and unknown meters, contractor permissions, installation confirmation, duplicate requests, manual-role restrictions, manual passwords, receipt verification and interrupted requests. These checks complement the scenario results rather than replacing their denominator.

A repeated submission for a connected meter returned its existing record without issuing another connection request. A repeated submission for a manual case was rejected. Both behaviours protect against unintended duplicate operational requests.
#### 4.5.4 Failure notification and manual completion
The twenty failed cases generated sixty HES notification records: twenty for each channel. This verifies record creation and routing. It does not demonstrate that a real HES officer received a message, because external delivery was simulated.
All twenty manual cases were subsequently completed with the HES officer role and a simulator-verified reference. The officer's password was checked again. An invented reference was rejected, and a contractor could not perform manual confirmation. Successful confirmation recorded the officer and receipt in the audit trail.
The simulator accepts its documented reference pattern only to demonstrate the workflow. That pattern is not operational proof. In live mode an authorised HES gateway must verify the completion reference; typed text or a checkbox alone cannot establish a real connection.

### 4.6 Browser mobile and deployment checks
Eleven browser checks passed in a separate local Chrome test process. They covered the register, three-day policy, notification states, password fields, identifier rejection, connection confirmation, manual handling, mobile layout, controlled camera denial and absence of uncaught runtime errors.
The scanner was checked at a 390 by 844 pixel viewport. It provides camera decoding, barcode-photograph upload and manual entry. Camera startup requests the rear device where supported. Stop, page-hide and visibility-change actions release camera resources. A delayed-start test stopped the scanner before the camera promise resolved; the late stream was then correctly stopped.
The new test file has 100 identifiers, each represented as Code 128 and QR. All 200 rendered symbols decoded to the expected value. Ten selected Code 128 images also passed through the browser's photograph-upload path and retrieved the correct records. These are digital image tests and do not replace printed-label tests on physical phones.
Two Streamlit checks passed: sign-in rendering and authenticated commissioning-console rendering. Streamlit uses the same process service and can share PostgreSQL with the Vercel application. It supports camera photographs and image upload, but its physical-camera operation and cloud deployment were not verified.

### 4.7 Objective 5 Framework validation and future state
The demonstrated future state assigns one current holder, verifies both workers at transfer, escalates holding beyond three days and begins commissioning from a validated scan. An unconfirmed operation has a manual HES route and notification records rather than an assumed success.
A three-day escalation rule does not guarantee completion within three days. Similarly, a simulator acknowledgement does not prove immediate live commissioning. Procurement constraints, network availability and manual fault-resolution time remain uncertainties. Any future-state financial calculation must state and test its assumptions.
No completed Delphi ratings or signed expert-consensus results were supplied for this rebuild. Objective 5 is not reported as fully validated. Experts should assess the time policy, responsibilities, notification workload, manual route and feasibility. The thesis should report actual panel size, rating distributions, disagreements and consensus rather than insert assumed approval.
A controlled pilot should measure custody completeness, handover delay, confirmed automatic connections, false connected states, message receipt, manual-resolution time and billing readiness. Real financial effects require independent billing reconciliation. The earlier projected 98% reduction is not retained as an achieved result.
### 4.8 Meaning of the 90 percent target
The selected functional tests and commissioning outcomes exceed the 90% acceptance threshold. They support the conclusion that the prototype follows the tested rules. They do not support renaming this result machine-learning accuracy or claiming that at least 90% of live meters will connect automatically.
Synthetic data alone do not explain why the previous prediction score was lower. Performance also depends on the target, features, noise, evaluation split and changes between periods. More importantly, predicting a long delay did not measure the clarified commissioning objective. The corrective action was to align the implementation and evaluation with that objective, not edit labels until a desired score appeared.
Passing designed examples can coexist with untested failures. These tests do not estimate how frequently faults occur in service. An operational evaluation should define acceptance criteria before running, retain failures in its denominator and include representative devices, departments and network conditions.
Useful acceptance criteria would combine correct end-to-end handling with zero false connected states, traceable custody, verified notification receipt and controlled manual resolution. The utility must approve the operational sample and thresholds. The local result alone cannot provide that approval.
### 4.9 Limitations
No authorised ZETDC HES, real meter, billing service or live recipient was used. PostgreSQL cloud deployment, actual message delivery, printed-label phone scanning, load performance and independent security testing remain unverified. The live HES adapter specifies a gateway contract that must be mapped to the vendor's authorised interface.
The rebuild validates identity, custody, account, CT ratio and installation confirmation. It does not independently verify installation photographs, GPS proximity, test certificates, SIM provisioning or radio signal as server-side evidence. Earlier claims that all these controls are already implemented must not be carried into this evaluation.
Production use also needs account lifecycle management, password recovery, access monitoring, backup and restore procedures and secure hosting. Notification status records distinguish simulated, queued, accepted and failed outcomes. Email receipt and WhatsApp delivery receipts require further integration and operational verification.
### 4.10 Chapter summary
The rebuild aligns the project with custody tracking and scan-triggered commissioning. Tracking passed 28/28 checks, commissioning passed 16/16, and all eighty scenarios reached their expected initial state. Sixty connected automatically in the simulator; twenty entered the manual queue and subsequently received verified manual simulator confirmations. The other core, browser, Streamlit and barcode checks also passed.
These results support continued prototype development and a controlled pilot. They do not demonstrate live utility commissioning, message receipt or realised savings. The 90% functional target was exceeded in the selected suite, while the automatic-connection rate in its deliberately mixed cases was 75%. Both measures are reported to preserve the distinction.
